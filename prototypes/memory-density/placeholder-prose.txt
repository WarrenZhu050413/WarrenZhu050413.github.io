It enters the present and changes how I experience it. I begin to think about what will remain of something before it has gone. And much of what I do seems to be an attempt to prevent that distance from opening in the first place.

When I listen to an old song, or return to one of these books, I recognize a feeling that is difficult to name. I am drawn to the sense that a whole world once existed around these words. There were people for whom it was simply the present. They did not encounter it as a memory. I can read what they left, but I cannot quite reach the ordinary life that surrounded it.

Perhaps this is why nostalgia does not seem like the right word. I do not especially want to live in the past. What unsettles me is that the present can become just as distant. The things that seem close and available now might one day have to be approached through a sentence, a photograph, or a song. I can understand this without wanting it. Knowing that it happens does not make me less afraid of it.
