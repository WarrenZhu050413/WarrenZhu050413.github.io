I've learned for myself that the sensibilities I have come from fear. I wish that things could not become dark.

Most of what I do is to make sure that I don't actually lose the present, that it's somehow there, and that it's kept there somehow.

I keep coming back to the fact that something can still be here while I am already afraid of losing it. The fear does not wait for anything to happen. It enters the present and changes how I experience it. I begin to think about what will remain of something before it has gone. And much of what I do seems to be an attempt to prevent that distance from opening in the first place.

I don't like looking at the past. That is why I grasp so much to the present and what I have and whom I love.

It is a kind of longing for the past. Or perhaps not longing exactly. It is experiencing the present through memory, while fearing that the present really will become a memory.

When I listen to an old song, or return to one of these books, I recognize a feeling that is difficult to name. I am drawn to the sense that a whole world once existed around these words. There were people for whom it was simply the present. They did not encounter it as a memory. I can read what they left, but I cannot quite reach the ordinary life that surrounded it.

Perhaps this is why nostalgia does not seem like the right word. I do not especially want to live in the past. What unsettles me is that the present can become just as distant. The things that seem close and available now might one day have to be approached through a sentence, a photograph, or a song. I can understand this without wanting it. Knowing that it happens does not make me less afraid of it.

There is something especially difficult about a world that I can see but cannot change. Literature lets me remain with it for a while. I can return to a passage, read it slowly, and find the same words waiting. But this kind of return has a limit. I am returning to what has been kept. The world itself does not become open to me again, however much I want to stay with it.

I think the same wish appears in parts of my life that seem quite different from reading. I want work to accumulate, for what I have done to become part of what I can do next. I want friendships to continue, and relationships to have somewhere to go. I want there to be a connection between one day and another. So much of what I call progress seems bound up with that desire for continuity.

It also helps me understand why I find certain brief experiences difficult to enjoy. If I believe something will not last, I can begin to withdraw from it before it has ended. Sometimes I cut it off myself. There is a contradiction there that I cannot explain away. I want to keep the present, yet the thought of its ending can make me less able to remain in it while it is still happening.

All of this (from literature I consume, movies that I like to watch, things I like to do, the need for my career to compound and go, for my friendships and relationships to stay and go on, to abruptly cut off engagements that I believe will not last) comes from this.

What I want to preserve is more than an account of what happened. I want the possibility of recognizing it again with someone, of saying that this is something we still know. A memory can feel less distant when it remains part of a shared present. I think that is why continuity matters so much to me. I want what has happened to stay involved in what happens next, rather than becoming something I can only look back at.

I do not have a conclusion that makes this fear disappear. I can see it in what I read and in the things I try to keep going. I can see that trying to hold the present is itself a way of living in it, even when it makes living more difficult. For now I am trying to describe that wish clearly: that what is here might stay close, and that it might not become dark.
